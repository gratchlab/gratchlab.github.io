CS 3891/5891:Affective Computing: Emotion, AI, and Human Behavior

Course Overview

How can AI understand and respond intelligently to human emotion? This interdisciplinary, project-based course brings together ideas from artificial intelligence, psychology, and human-centered design to explore how emotion shapes human behavior and how computational systems can recognize, model, and influence it. Students will learn core theories of emotion alongside modern AI methods, including emotion recognition and generative AI, and apply them to problems such as emotionally intelligent chatbots, adaptive recommender systems, mental health and education technologies, and human-AI interaction. Working in interdisciplinary teams, students will identify a real-world problem, propose and pitch an affective computing solution, build a prototype using existing AI tools, and design an evaluation to determine whether it works.

Advanced programming is not required; successful teams will combine computational skills with expertise in emotion, human behavior, experimental design, and creative application development.

Background / Prerequisites

Students should have prior preparation through one of two routes.

Computer science students should have substantial programming experience (e.g., CS 2201, CS 2204 or equivalent); prior coursework in artificial intelligence or machine learning is recommended.

Psychology/cognitive science students should have completed an introductory statistics course (e.g., PSY 2100 or PSY-PC 2110) and a course in experimental design or research methods (PSY 2150, PSY-PC 2170 or PSY-PC 2180, or equivalent). More advanced statistics coursework (e.g., PSY-PC 2120) would be beneficial.
